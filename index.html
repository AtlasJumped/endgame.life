The vision

Our picture of about 2036

Our vision, not fact. Nothing on this page is a forecast or a promise. It is where we think this could go.

A microphone button that never goes away

In the bottom left corner of every phone, always, is a microphone button. Press it and talk. It goes straight to your own AI agent.

Your agent learns you, in your own database

It records what you say and learns about you. The record is stored in a database that belongs to you, not to an app company.

Danger assets stay behind a physical key

Money is the main danger asset. We picture a hardware key that you carry, or keep in a lockbox at home, with a manual switch. Nothing that moves money works until you flip it. Try the switch.

Money is locked. No agent can move it.

Youvoice, habits, places match
Fakedoes not match

Oversharing with the right ally protects you

Our idea is that if your agent holds your voice and your activity, it can tell the real you from a fake, so a stolen ID matters much less once money is locked. Whether this holds up against fast-improving fakes is unknown. It needs testing.

A home that runs itself

Every light and every plug is automated. The thermostat, the door locks, the garage door, even the ceiling fans answer to your dashboard. Lights and heating turn off when nobody needs them, which saves money. Air filters check the air quality and switch on by themselves, which protects your health. Cameras are there only if you choose them.

Fewer screens, more faces

A watch or a bracelet does what a phone does, so more of us leave the phone at home. We hope the time saved goes to looking at each other.

Why this is urgent

Phone AI may soon be controlled by a few companies. If that happens before an open picture exists, the open-source community loses its grip. We want to share this vision first, and build it together as an open-source community.

Why not now?

There is no good reason not to have this today.

This vision is not far off. It is around the corner. The tools mostly exist. The only thing missing is people building it together, in the open.

We describe the absolute endgame of what AI can do at its best, and then we say: we are really not that far. It is built as a ten-year vision. If everybody jumps on it today, it is a one-year vision.

Imagine

Imagine the world's best engineer and the world's best designer took all of humanity's tools and made the one best tool for you. What would it be?

We think it would be a dashboard: one place you open on your phone or your computer, a one-stop shop for everything you want.

And it still would not be as good as what the whole world could make together in an open-source effort. The world's best engineer is all of us.

What "everything you want" means

  • Control over your time
  • Control over your money
  • Your family and relationships
  • Your power: where you stand, how to grow it, how to protect it
  • Fun
  • Good health
  • High productivity
  • More happiness, and less suffering, per hour
  • Less time on things you do not enjoy
  • More confidence that your actions are the best ones for your mission

Why it must be open source

If someone takes this over and tries to monetize it, it loses its open-source nature, and that corrupts it.

This tool needs to be an open source tool. Free forever, no ads, no in-app purchases.

If this is the first place you have seen this idea, share it.

What is blocking us

  • Dashboards today are made for one field of work, or they cost money, or they are free with in-app purchases.
  • They are limited by the few engineers building them.
  • Phone AI may soon be controlled by a few companies.
  • Safe, read-only access is not yet standard at banks and apps.
  • Not enough of us are building it yet.

An honest note

This is a vision, not a promise.

The people are made up. The screens are storyboards, not screenshots. Here is where things stand.

Real today

  • A free dashboard at personaldash.life: text you paste into an AI assistant, which then sets it up.
  • A setup rating from 1 to 100, based on what the assistant can read.
  • Tabs for today, agenda, inbox, messages, money, fun, family and friends, work, and you.
  • Rules: you are in charge, nothing leaves, read-only money.

Hoped for

  • One tap to connect most services.
  • Standard read-only access at banks and apps.
  • Drafts that sound like you.
  • Shared plans among people who opt in.
  • Employers and people agreeing on a privacy firewall.

Unsolved

  • How to share dashboards safely when they touch work data.
  • Security risks, including malicious files and tricked assistants.
  • Whether assistants stay accurate enough to trust with drafts.
  • Whether companies will allow it.
